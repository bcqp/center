Mission and vision

What the series is for, and the standard it holds itself to.

Mission

BCQP's mission is to advance quantitative economics, econometrics, statistical sciences and evidence-based public policy through independent research, transparent empirical analysis, open methodological resources and research capacity development. The center seeks to make rigorous quantitative work accessible to researchers, practitioners and policy audiences without weakening the standards required for credible inference.

Vision

BCQP aims to contribute to a research environment in which economic and policy claims can be traced to documented data, evaluated through appropriate methods, reproduced where possible and communicated with their uncertainty and limitations made explicit.

Institutional objectives

  • Produce empirical research on macroeconomic, financial, labor, development, climate and public-policy questions.
  • Advance econometric and statistical methods, including time-series, panel, Bayesian, causal and computational approaches.
  • Document data sources, definitions, transformations, revisions and validation procedures.
  • Support reproducible research through accessible code, model documentation and replication materials where data permissions allow.
  • Develop research training in econometrics, statistics, quantitative policy analysis and computational methods.
  • Encourage scholarly exchange and collaboration while preserving research independence.

Operating commitments

Analytical integrity

Methods must fit the research question, and conclusions must not exceed what the empirical design supports.

Transparency

Research outputs should identify sources, methods, assumptions, limitations and material changes.

Accessibility

Findings should be communicated clearly, with technical detail available for scrutiny.

Independence

Research conclusions are not determined by funders, data providers or intended audiences.

Scope and institutional status

BCQP is an independent research initiative. These statements describe its research purpose and operating commitments; they do not imply government status, statutory authority, accreditation or affiliation with a university or international organisation. Formal programmes and partnerships will be identified publicly only after they are established.
