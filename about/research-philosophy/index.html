Research philosophy

How we think about evidence, models and uncertainty.

Measurement before modelling

Empirical analysis begins with the statistical object: what is measured, for whom, over which period, at what frequency and under which definition. Researchers should inspect source metadata, base years, classification systems, coverage, seasonal adjustment and revision practices before selecting an estimator. Where a series changes definition or base, the treatment of the break must be documented.

Methods follow the estimand

Model choice begins with the question and the target quantity to be estimated. Dynamic time-series models can describe temporal interactions; panel methods can exploit variation across units and time; causal designs require a defensible identification strategy; Bayesian and structural models make assumptions explicit; and machine-learning methods can support prediction or flexible pattern detection. These approaches answer different questions and should not be treated as interchangeable.

Identification and interpretation

Every empirical design should state the source of identifying variation, the assumptions required for interpretation and the threats that remain. Predictive accuracy is not, by itself, evidence of a causal effect. A dynamic response is conditional on the model's specification and identifying restrictions.

Uncertainty and robustness

Uncertainty is part of the result. Where appropriate, outputs should report standard errors or posterior intervals, sensitivity to lag length and specification, alternative samples, diagnostic checks and the consequences of data revisions. Robustness analysis should be informative rather than a search for a preferred result.

Reproducibility and data provenance

Research documentation should record source institution, dataset or table, retrieval date, units, transformations, missing-data treatment, sample period, software and package versions, model specification and estimation settings. Replication materials should be provided when licensing, confidentiality and data-access conditions permit.

Communication and policy relevance

Technical results should be translated into clear statements about magnitude, uncertainty, distribution and policy relevance. Reports should distinguish descriptive evidence, model-based interpretation, causal estimates and scenarios. Policy implications should be linked to the evidence and should state the conditions under which they may change.

Limitations are reported

BIQEP outputs should identify data constraints, measurement error, model dependence, external-validity limits and any unresolved uncertainty material to interpretation. A limitation is not hidden because it complicates the narrative; it is part of the evidence record.
