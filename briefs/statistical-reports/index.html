Statistical reports

Documented datasets and statistical tables derived from official releases.

Purpose and scope

BCQP statistical reports present documented statistical tables, indicator compilations and quantitative summaries derived from identifiable source releases. A report should make the path from the original observation to the displayed statistic transparent. It should not imply that a derived table is an official statistic produced or endorsed by the original source agency.

Required documentation

  • Source institution, publication, dataset and table identifier.
  • Direct source link, retrieval date and release vintage.
  • Indicator definitions, units, frequency, geographic and population coverage.
  • Transformations, aggregation rules, deflators, exchange-rate conversions and seasonal adjustments.
  • Missing-data treatment, interpolation or imputation flags, and any exclusions.
  • Revisions, breaks in series, classification changes and comparability warnings.
  • Calculation code or formulas and a versioned output where possible.

Official sources and metadata

Bangladesh source tables should be traced to the responsible statistical producer, including Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics, Bangladesh Bank and the Finance Division. The IMF Dissemination Standards Bulletin Board and UN National Quality Assurance Framework provide useful metadata and quality-assurance references. These frameworks guide documentation; their mention does not mean a BCQP report has been formally assessed or certified under them.

Statistical processing and derived values

Derived indicators should show the formula or transformation used. For example, a year-on-year growth rate should identify the numerator, comparison period and treatment of revised values. Real values should state the deflator and base year. Ratios should define numerator and denominator. Interpolated or imputed values must be labelled and should never be presented as directly observed official figures.

Quality checks

Before publication, tables should be checked for units, duplicate observations, date alignment, missing periods, implausible discontinuities, arithmetic consistency and agreement with the cited source. Where totals are expected to reconcile with components, any residual should be explained. Quality checks should be recorded, not merely asserted.

International comparability

For cross-country work, metadata should be checked alongside values. The World Development Indicators, IMF Data, ILOSTAT and UN SDG metadata can support comparative analysis, but national definitions, reference years and revisions may differ. A common indicator name is not sufficient evidence of comparability.

Interpretation and limitations

Statistical reports describe measurements and derived quantities. They do not establish causal mechanisms unless accompanied by a separate research design. Each report should state uncertainty, data gaps and limits to interpretation, including any dependence on assumptions used in constructing the series.
