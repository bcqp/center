Energy use and electricity in Bangladesh

Peer benchmarks and 5 model specifications on 12 variables (1991–2024) for electricity, energy mix, imports and growth. Bangladesh is below the peer median and worsening on 3 of 5 indicators judged.

Tuhin G. M. Al Mamun and Abdur Rahim · Statistical report · 2026-11-14

Cite as: Al Mamun, Tuhin G. M., and Abdur Rahim (2026). Energy use and electricity in Bangladesh. BIQEP Policy Briefs. Archived at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24215521.

Abstract. How does Bangladesh's energy and electricity use compare with peers, and how dependent is the economy on fossil fuels? We benchmark Bangladesh against India, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, Viet Nam, Indonesia and Nepal using World Bank data, and estimate a 12-variable Bayesian VAR with vector autoregression and local-projection checks on annual data for 1991–2024. Of 5 estimated impulse responses, 0 have a band that excludes zero at the peak. Where the models cannot separate the effect from zero, we say so.

Key findings

  • Electric power use, kWh per head: Bangladesh 632 (2024), peer median 959; rank 5 of 7; change over ten years +307 (peer median +331).
  • Energy use, kg oil equivalent per head: Bangladesh 288 (2024), peer median 614; rank 7 of 7; change over ten years +66.11 (peer median +92.42).
  • Fossil fuels, % of energy use: Bangladesh 81.21 (2024), peer median 66.80; rank 6 of 7; change over ten years +6.09 (peer median -1.14).
  • GDP per head, constant US$: Bangladesh 1985 (2025), peer median 3416; rank 5 of 7; change over ten years +760 (peer median +660).
  • Access to electricity, % of population: Bangladesh 99.50 (2024), peer median 99.90; rank 5 of 7; change over ten years +37.10 (peer median +6.45).
  • Models: the response of real GDP growth to a shock in electricity use per head, growth is of the same sign across specifications; 0 of 5 bands exclude zero at the peak (Table 1).

1. Question

How does Bangladesh's energy and electricity use compare with peers, and how dependent is the economy on fossil fuels? We answer with official data, benchmarking against six regional peers and several estimators that make different assumptions; the results are reported whatever they show.

2. Data

Annual series from the World Bank World Development Indicators for Bangladesh, retrieved on 2026-11-14, years 1991–2024 (34 observations after transformation). The 12 variables are: Electricity use per head, growth (%); Real GDP growth (%); CPI inflation (%); Energy use per head, growth (%); Change in fossil share of energy (pp); Change in imports/GDP (pp); Depreciation of the Taka (%); Change in investment/GDP (pp); Change in manufacturing/GDP (pp); Change in exports/GDP (pp); Change in broad money/GDP (pp); Change in private credit/GDP (pp). Transformations: growth rates and percentage changes where the series is a level, first differences where it is a ratio.

Validation. Single compiled source for most series. Real GDP growth and CPI inflation were compared with the IMF DataMapper series for 2015–2025 (IMF: NGDP_RPCH, PCPIPCH); see the data-validation note in the Statistical report on inflation, the Taka and policy stance for the gaps found (up to 0.05 percentage points for GDP growth and 1.6 for inflation). Gaps in peer data are shown with the year used.

4. Empirical strategy

We trace the response of real GDP growth to a shock in electricity use per head, growth. Models, all run in Open Econometric Systems: (i) a Bayesian VAR with a Minnesota prior on 12 variables (shock ordered first), with a variation in lag length and prior tightness; (ii) a four-variable VAR with a recursive ordering; (iii) local projections with Newey–West standard errors. The large system is possible only in the Bayesian model: an unrestricted VAR on 12 variables with two lags has 25 coefficients per equation for about 34 usable observations, leaving too few degrees of freedom for reliable estimates. Recursive identification is an assumption; the responses are dynamic correlations conditional on the ordering, not causal effects.

6. Policy assessment

Where the record supports criticism

Bangladesh is more than 5% below the peer median, and its ten-year change is worse than the peer median change.

  1. Electric power use, kWh per head: Bangladesh 632 (2024), peer median 959; rank 5 of 7; change over ten years +307 (peer median +331).
  2. Energy use, kg oil equivalent per head: Bangladesh 288 (2024), peer median 614; rank 7 of 7; change over ten years +66.11 (peer median +92.42).
  3. Fossil fuels, % of energy use: Bangladesh 81.21 (2024), peer median 66.80; rank 6 of 7; change over ten years +6.09 (peer median -1.14).

Behind peers, but improving

  • GDP per head, constant US$: Bangladesh 1985 (2025), peer median 3416; rank 5 of 7; change over ten years +760 (peer median +660).

Where Bangladesh does well

  • Access to electricity, % of population: Bangladesh 99.50 (2024), peer median 99.90; rank 5 of 7; change over ten years +37.10 (peer median +6.45).

Where the evidence is not strong enough

  • Dynamic effects: 0 of 5 impulse-response bands exclude zero, so the estimated effect of electricity use per head, growth on real GDP growth cannot be stated precisely with annual data.

Implications for discussion

Judgements drawn from the record, not estimates.

  • Electricity use per head is well below India, Indonesia and Vietnam; the investment gap in generation and grids should be costed in public plans.
  • The fossil share of energy use should be reported with import cost data, since it determines exposure to price and exchange-rate shocks.

7. Limitations

  • Annual data give about 34 observations; responses at one to six years are fragile.
  • Recursive identification is an assumption, not a result.
  • Peer comparisons use each country's latest year, which differs for some indicators; ten-year changes use the year ten years before the latest.
  • Peer groups reflect economies of comparable region or size and are a choice; other groups could change ranks.
