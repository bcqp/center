Quantitative economics · Econometric research · Public policy

Understanding economic and social policy through quantitative evidence.

BIQEP is an independent institute advancing quantitative economics, econometrics, statistical sciences and evidence-informed public policy. We combine theoretical and applied research, statistical analysis, mathematical and computational methods, data development, and policy evaluation to understand economic and social systems. Our work spans Bangladesh and internationally relevant questions.

Our activities include original research, policy analysis, methodological development, open analytical tools, scholarly exchange and professional training. Bangladesh is a central focus, while our methods and research agenda engage with wider global debates.

By Dr. Tuhin G. M. Al Mamun, Co-Founder

What BIQEP does

From economic questions to measurable policy insight.

Public policy is shaped by interacting forces: prices, wages, credit, public spending, institutions, technology, energy systems and household decisions. BIQEP brings quantitative methods to these connections so that policy debates can move beyond isolated indicators and unsupported explanations.

01

Measure the problem

Build reliable indicators, examine trends and distributions, identify structural changes, and assess how outcomes differ across households, sectors, regions and time.

02

Explain the mechanisms

Use econometric models, causal designs and structural analysis to investigate the channels linking economic shocks, institutions and policy interventions to observed outcomes.

03

Evaluate policy choices

Estimate effects, compare scenarios, examine distributional consequences and assess uncertainty so decision-makers can understand the likely trade-offs.

04

Translate evidence

Present findings in policy briefs, statistical reports and accessible visualisations, distinguishing robust results from assumptions and unresolved questions.

How we use quantitative methods

Methods are selected to answer the policy question.

No single estimator can answer every economic question. Our analytical approach matches the method to the data structure, the mechanism under investigation and the estimand that matters for policy.

Time-series and macroeconomic models

Analyse inflation, monetary transmission, exchange rates, fiscal dynamics and business cycles using dynamic regression, cointegration, VAR/VECM, state-space and structural macroeconomic models.

Panel data and comparative analysis

Compare countries, sectors or regions while accounting for unobserved heterogeneity, common shocks, cross-sectional dependence and dynamic adjustment.

Causal inference and programme evaluation

Investigate policy effects using transparent identification strategies, treatment-effect estimators, difference-in-differences, event studies and related quasi-experimental designs where the data support them.

Distributional and social analysis

Study who gains and who bears the costs through subgroup analysis, inequality measures, quantile methods, labour-market analysis and household-level evidence.

Forecasting and machine learning

Use predictive models for forecasting, classification and pattern detection, with out-of-sample validation and a clear distinction between prediction and causal explanation.

Scenario and structural modelling

Use structural models and simulation to explore counterfactual policy scenarios, transmission mechanisms and possible outcomes under alternative assumptions.

Policy questions, not methods for their own sake

What do the numbers mean for people, firms and institutions?

A model result becomes useful when it helps explain a real decision. We connect macroeconomic stability with household purchasing power, labour-market opportunities, access to finance, public-service provision and the distribution of transition costs.

Examples of questions we investigate

  • How do exchange-rate movements pass through to domestic prices?
  • Do changes in policy rates transmit to lending, investment and output?
  • How do inflation and fiscal adjustments affect households differently?
  • What are the employment and distributional effects of energy transition?
  • Which factors constrain productivity, investment and inclusive growth?
  • How resilient are public finances and external balances to economic shocks?
A transparent research process

Every conclusion should be traceable to evidence.

Our work is organised around a reproducible sequence: define the policy question, document the data, specify the empirical strategy, estimate and diagnose the model, test sensitivity, interpret the magnitude and uncertainty, and communicate the policy implications.

01

Question and estimand

Define the policy problem, target population, outcome of interest, time horizon and quantity the analysis seeks to estimate.

02

Data construction and validation

Use documented official and scholarly sources; record units, definitions, revisions, missing observations, transformations and breaks in measurement.

03

Model specification and identification

Explain why the chosen model fits the question, state assumptions, document identifying variation and assess whether the design supports descriptive, predictive or causal interpretation.

04

Diagnostics and robustness

Evaluate model fit and assumptions, examine alternative specifications, test sensitivity to sample and measurement choices, and report uncertainty.

05

Policy interpretation

Translate estimated magnitudes into economic meaning, identify affected groups and sectors, explain trade-offs and distinguish evidence from recommendations.

Academic publishing · Forthcoming

A journal for quantitative economics and public policy.

BIQEP is developing a proposed peer-reviewed journal under the working title Journal of Quantitative Economics and Public Policy (JQEP) that will invite original research submissions from scholars and practitioners worldwide, regardless of institutional affiliation.

BIQEP's policy briefs remain institute-led research outputs. JQEP will operate separately as an open-submission scholarly journal; its title is provisional, no ISSN has been assigned, and submissions are not yet open.
