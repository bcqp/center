How a brief is made

From official data to estimates: how series are sourced, checked and modelled.

How a BIQEP brief is developed

Each brief begins with a defined policy question and a clear statement of what the analysis is intended to establish. The workflow links source selection, statistical validation, analytical design, estimation, interpretation and publication review. The objective is traceable evidence: readers should be able to identify where a figure came from, how it was processed, what the model estimates and which conclusions the design can support.

01

Define the question and estimand

Specify the policy problem, unit of analysis, population, outcome, exposure or treatment, time horizon and target quantity. Distinguish a descriptive indicator, predictive target, causal effect and structural counterfactual before choosing a method.

02

Identify and document the data

Prioritise the original statistical producer and record the dataset, table, definition, unit, frequency, reference period, release date and retrieval date. For Bangladesh, source discovery includes Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics, Bangladesh Bank and the Finance Division. The IMF Dissemination Standards Bulletin Board provides country metadata and dissemination context.

03

Validate and prepare the series

Match observations to source releases, verify units and periods, inspect revisions and structural breaks, and document transformations, missing-data treatment and sample construction. Statistical quality frameworks such as the UN National Quality Assurance Framework provide useful principles for transparent source and process documentation.

04

Select the analytical strategy

Choose a model that corresponds to the estimand and data-generating structure. Time-series, panel, causal, predictive, Bayesian and structural approaches answer different questions. State identifying assumptions, lag structure, priors or restrictions, diagnostics and uncertainty measures before interpreting estimates.

05

Estimate, diagnose and test sensitivity

Run the stated specification, assess convergence or optimisation status where relevant, inspect residual and stability diagnostics, and conduct sensitivity checks targeted at plausible threats. For predictive tasks, use validation that respects time order or group structure. For causal claims, evaluate the design's identifying assumptions.

06

Interpret and communicate

Translate estimates into meaningful units, explain the time horizon and affected groups, and distinguish empirical findings from mechanisms and policy judgement. Report uncertainty and limitations. Forecasts and scenarios must state their assumptions and should not be presented as observed outcomes.

07

Publish and maintain the record

Provide source references, methods, data cut-off, model settings and replication materials where permitted. Correct material errors transparently and record the date and nature of revisions under the Corrections Policy.

Analytical design: match the method to the estimand

BIQEP distinguishes descriptive measurement, prediction and causal or structural analysis. A predictive model can forecast an outcome without identifying a policy effect; a causal design estimates an effect under explicit identifying assumptions; a structural model supports counterfactual analysis conditional on its behavioural and equilibrium assumptions. The estimand, sample, unit of observation, time dimension and identifying variation should be stated in each output.

Time-series analysis

Methods may include dynamic regression, ARDL and error-correction models, VAR/VECM, local projections, state-space models and structural time-series approaches. Integration properties, cointegration, stability, residual dependence and lag choice are assessed as relevant.

Panel-data analysis

Specifications consider unit and time effects, dynamic adjustment, slope heterogeneity, cross-sectional dependence, common shocks and missing observations. Estimator choice should reflect the panel's dimensions and dependence structure.

Causal and programme evaluation

Difference-in-differences, event studies, instrumental variables, regression discontinuity and synthetic-control designs are considered when treatment assignment and identifying assumptions can be stated and assessed.

Prediction and machine learning

Predictive performance is assessed with validation designs that respect temporal ordering, grouped observations and leakage risks. Predictive accuracy is not presented as evidence of a causal effect.

Bayesian and structural modelling

Bayesian models document likelihoods, priors, posterior computation and convergence diagnostics. Structural models state mechanisms, equilibrium conditions, calibration or estimation strategies and counterfactual assumptions.

Robustness and uncertainty

Checks probe plausible threats rather than search mechanically for significance. Results report uncertainty measures, economically meaningful magnitudes and material sensitivity to specifications.

International statistical and methodological references

  • IMF statistical manuals and guidance provide concepts and compilation guidance for macroeconomic statistics.
  • Government Finance Statistics Manual provides a framework for fiscal statistics and public-sector analysis.
  • UN SDG metadata documents indicator definitions, methodologies and custodianship.
  • Statistical Data and Metadata eXchange (SDMX) provides standards for exchanging statistical data and metadata.

These resources inform definitions and documentation; they do not certify an individual BIQEP estimate. Each output remains responsible for its own data provenance, model specification, diagnostics and interpretation.

What every brief reports

  • Research question, estimand, unit of analysis and sample period.
  • Data sources, release and retrieval dates, definitions and transformations.
  • Model specification, identification assumptions and diagnostic checks.
  • Uncertainty, robustness results and relevant data or model limitations.
  • Clear distinction between observed evidence, estimated relationships, causal conclusions and policy recommendations.
