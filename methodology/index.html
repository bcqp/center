Methodology

How each brief is made

A short, fixed process. The point is that a reader can see where every number came from and repeat the analysis.

Topic

A priority topic is chosen. News and market commentary can suggest a question. They are never used as evidence.

Official data

Series come from Bangladesh Bank, the Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics, the IMF and the World Bank. The source, address and retrieval date of each series are recorded in the Data hub.

Validation

Every figure is checked against its original release. When two sources disagree, the disagreement is resolved against the original announcement, or the figure is left out. Gaps are never filled with guesses.

Estimation

Models run in Open Econometric Systems (OES), an open-source engine with vector autoregressions, local projections, structural and Bayesian models. The specification, sample and settings are published with the brief.

Review and approval

A draft is prepared with automated tools, then read, checked and approved by the author. Nothing is published before that.

Corrections

If a figure is found not to match its official source, it is corrected, and the correction is noted on the page with its date.

What a brief always states

  • the data used, with source and retrieval date;
  • the sample period and any break in the series, such as the 2023 change of CPI base;
  • the model, the identifying assumptions and the main robustness checks;
  • what the results cannot show, including causal claims the design does not support.

Open tools

The estimation engine is open source. You can run the same models in your browser at Open Econometric Systems, or read the code on GitHub.
