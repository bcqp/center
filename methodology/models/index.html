Open Econometric Systems

Open Econometric Systems (OES) is a founder-developed research infrastructure initiative of the Bangladesh Institute of Quantitative Economics and Public Policy (BIQEP). OES was initiated and developed by BIQEP's Founder and Executive Director, Dr. Tuhin G. M. Al Mamun. It is an open-source econometric platform designed to expand access to quantitative research methods through browser-based statistical computation.

OES forms part of BIQEP's commitment to open research infrastructure, methodological innovation and quantitative capacity development. Its estimator implementations are written in Python. The current BIQEP site description lists 69 models; users should consult the live OES catalogue for the latest implemented model count and availability.

Browser-based execution is intended to keep data processing on the user's device. Users should still review the platform's current technical documentation, browser requirements and data-handling behaviour before using sensitive or restricted datasets.

Model families

Time series
OLS with diagnostics, ARDL bounds tests, nonlinear ARDL, VAR, VECM, instrumental variables and GMM, local projections, structural VARs, structural-break tests, threshold and smooth-transition regression, ARIMA, GARCH, quantile regression, cointegration tests and wavelet coherence.
Panel data
Pooled, fixed- and random-effects models, dynamic panel GMM, mean-group and common-correlated-effects estimators, panel local projections, panel threshold models and spatial panels.
Causal inference
Difference-in-differences, event studies, interrupted time series, regression discontinuity, synthetic control, matching and shift-share instruments.
Machine learning
LASSO, ridge, elastic net, random forest, XGBoost and double machine learning.
Bayesian
Bayesian regression and VARs with Minnesota and other priors, factor-augmented, time-varying and stochastic-volatility VARs, sign-restricted VARs and Bayesian local projections.
Markov switching
Markov-switching regression, autoregressions and VARs, including time-varying transition probabilities.

Choosing a model for the research question

Model choice follows the estimand, data structure and identifying assumptions—not simply the availability of an estimator. A forecasting task requires out-of-sample validation; a causal question requires a defensible identification strategy; a long-run relationship requires attention to integration and cointegration properties; and a panel application must address heterogeneity and dependence where relevant.

Describe and diagnose

Begin with data summaries, plots, missingness, outliers, unit-root and break diagnostics as appropriate. Document transformations and the effective sample.

Estimate and validate

Record the exact model, options, lag order, priors or restrictions, optimisation settings and uncertainty calculation. Assess convergence and diagnostics where applicable.

Interpret conditionally

Explain what the estimator identifies under its assumptions. Do not infer causality from predictive fit or a statistically significant association alone.

Reproduce

Preserve the dataset source, code version, model settings and outputs. See the replication guidance.

Methodological references

For macroeconomic concepts and compilation, consult the IMF statistical manuals. For fiscal series, consult the Government Finance Statistics Manual. For data documentation, see SDMX and the UN National Quality Assurance Framework. These resources support methodological context; they do not certify OES implementations or BIQEP estimates.

Model groups and implementations should be checked against the current OES repository. DSGE models are not part of OES and are solved separately.
