Finance and credit

Bank lending, interest rates and financial stability.

Why finance and credit matter

Financial intermediation affects the allocation of savings, working capital, investment and household consumption. Credit conditions transmit monetary policy to the real economy, while asset quality, liquidity and capital conditions influence the resilience of banks and the supply of finance. Aggregate indicators should therefore be interpreted alongside institutional and sectoral heterogeneity.

Research questions

  • How do lending rates, deposit rates and credit volumes respond to policy and liquidity conditions?
  • How is credit distributed across sectors, firm types and regions?
  • How do non-performing loans, provisioning, capital and liquidity indicators evolve?
  • Are credit responses nonlinear across risk, capitalization or funding conditions?
  • How do financial conditions affect investment, employment and firm performance?

Data and measurement

Bangladesh Bank's Bangladesh Bank, Economic Data includes monetary and banking indicators, while its scheduled-bank statistics and financial-stability publications provide additional system information. The Bangladesh National Summary Data Page offers a harmonised entry point to monetary aggregates and interest-rate data. IMF metadata can help identify definitions and dissemination arrangements [IMF Dissemination Standards Bulletin Board].

Researchers should distinguish gross from net credit, nominal from real credit growth, scheduled-bank from broader financial-system coverage, and classified loans from broader measures of impaired assets. Bank-level ratios require consistent numerator and denominator definitions and attention to mergers, reporting changes and survivorship.

Analytical framework

BIQEP studies the bank-lending channel, interest-rate pass-through, credit allocation, financial stability and the interaction between bank balance sheets and macroeconomic shocks. A useful design separates the price of credit, quantity supplied, quantity demanded and realized credit quality.

Methods and identification

  • Bank-level panel models: heterogeneous responses across institutions, with bank and time effects where appropriate.
  • Threshold and nonlinear models: regime dependence across capitalization, liquidity or risk conditions.
  • Quantile methods: heterogeneity across the conditional distribution of credit or risk outcomes.
  • VAR and local projections: aggregate dynamics between policy, credit and macroeconomic outcomes.

Bank credit is jointly determined by supply and demand. Causal claims about credit supply require an identification strategy that separates these forces, such as plausibly exogenous funding or regulatory variation. Panel inference should account for serial dependence, common shocks and cross-sectional dependence where relevant.

Policy relevance

Findings may inform monetary transmission, prudential supervision, credit allocation and financial inclusion. Research should avoid treating aggregate credit expansion as equivalent to productive investment or improved access; allocation, pricing and repayment performance also matter.
