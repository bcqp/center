Research area

Growth and structure

Investment, savings, sector shares and urbanisation.

Why growth and structural transformation matter

Long-run development depends not only on aggregate output growth but also on productivity, capital accumulation, sectoral reallocation, technological adoption, trade integration and the quality of institutions. A country can experience rising GDP while productivity growth remains uneven across sectors or while gains are concentrated in a limited set of activities.

Research questions

  • How do investment, saving and productivity contribute to output growth?
  • Is labor and capital moving toward higher-productivity sectors?
  • How do trade openness, export composition and imported inputs relate to productivity?
  • What explains differences in growth and structural change across regions or peer economies?
  • How do human capital, infrastructure and institutional conditions shape long-run performance?

Data and measurement

National accounts and sectoral statistics are sourced from Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics (BBS); the IMF's Bangladesh metadata documents national-accounts compilation and source practices [IMF, Bangladesh national-accounts metadata]. International and long-run comparisons may draw on World Bank, World Development Indicators, but GDP rebasing, purchasing-power conversions, sector classifications and revisions must be considered before comparing growth or productivity levels.

Analytical framework

BIQEP separates aggregate growth accounting, sectoral reallocation and within-sector productivity change. Investment ratios, capital formation, savings, labor productivity, total factor productivity and export complexity measure different dimensions. Each project should specify the concept being measured and the assumptions behind constructed productivity estimates.

Methods and identification

  • Growth accounting: decomposition of output growth into factor accumulation and productivity components, conditional on production-function assumptions.
  • Sectoral shift-share decomposition: separates within-sector productivity change from reallocation effects.
  • Panel and convergence models: comparative dynamics with attention to common shocks and parameter heterogeneity.
  • VAR and local projections: dynamic interactions among investment, saving, productivity and output.

Growth regressions are sensitive to sample selection, measurement error, endogeneity and model specification. Cross-country panel analysis should assess cross-sectional dependence and slope heterogeneity. Accounting decompositions are descriptive unless supported by a separate identification strategy.

Policy relevance

Evidence can inform infrastructure priorities, investment climate, export diversification, skills, technology adoption and productivity policy. Policy conclusions should identify whether the binding issue is capital accumulation, labor productivity, sectoral reallocation or institutional constraints.
