Research agenda

Nine areas of work, from inflation and monetary policy to climate and energy.

The research agenda

BIQEP's research programme examines economic performance, structural change and public-policy outcomes through quantitative economics, econometrics and statistical analysis. Bangladesh is a central empirical focus, while comparative work is undertaken where definitions, coverage and time periods support meaningful comparison. The nine areas below are connected: shocks in prices, finance, trade, energy and public budgets can affect employment, household welfare and long-run development.

Cross-cutting research themes

Macroeconomic stability and distribution

Inflation, monetary and fiscal adjustment, exchange-rate movements and financial conditions are studied alongside real wages, household purchasing power and differences across population groups.

Structural transformation and productivity

Research considers sectoral change, investment, trade, firm dynamics, labour reallocation, human capital and the institutional conditions associated with productivity growth.

Climate, energy and social outcomes

Energy prices, decarbonisation and climate-related investment are connected to employment, affordability, public finance, regional exposure and distributional effects.

Financial systems and development

Credit allocation, banking conditions, financial inclusion and external financing are examined in relation to investment, resilience and economic opportunity.

Research design and evidence standards

Each project should define its research question and estimand before selecting a model. Descriptive monitoring, forecasting, structural interpretation and causal evaluation are distinct tasks and require different assumptions. Empirical work should document sample construction, variable definitions, transformations, missing-data treatment, model specification and uncertainty. Where identification is claimed, the source of identifying variation and the assumptions needed for causal interpretation must be explicit.

For Bangladesh macroeconomic aggregates, the national statistical system includes BBS, Bangladesh Bank and the Ministry of Finance among the key producers and disseminators of official statistics. The IMF's country metadata describes source agencies and compilation practices for individual statistical domains [IMF, Bangladesh DQAF; IMF Dissemination Standards Bulletin Board]. The Bangladesh Bank's NSDP provides access to core macroeconomic and financial domains, including national accounts, CPI, government operations, monetary aggregates, interest rates and balance of payments [Bangladesh Bank, NSDP; Bangladesh National Summary Data Page].

Comparative and international context

International comparisons draw, where appropriate, on the World Bank, World Development Indicators, IMF Data, ILO, ILOSTAT and UN SDG indicator metadata. A shared indicator label does not guarantee comparability: BIQEP checks definitions, units, reference periods, population coverage, revisions and methodological breaks before combining or comparing series. The IMF's metadata and dissemination framework provides country-level documentation that helps users assess statistical coverage and compilation practices [IMF, DSBB; IMF Dissemination Standards Bulletin Board].

Research outputs

  • Empirical research papers and working papers.
  • Policy briefs and analytical notes.
  • Data documentation, indicator notes and validation records.
  • Replication files and computational materials where licensing and access conditions permit.

Each output should distinguish source facts, estimated results, interpretation and policy implications. Findings are conditional on the data and model used; limitations and uncertainty are reported rather than suppressed. See the methodology, Data Hub and research policies.
