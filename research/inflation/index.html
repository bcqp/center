Research area

Inflation

Price dynamics, food and non-food inflation, and expectations.

Why inflation matters

Inflation affects real purchasing power, household consumption, firms' input costs, wage bargaining and the real value of nominal contracts. For Bangladesh, analysis needs to distinguish headline, food and non-food price movements and to examine how exchange-rate changes, import costs, administered prices, domestic demand and expectations interact. The research question is not simply whether prices rose, but which components moved, for whom, and through which transmission channels.

Research questions

  • How do food and non-food components contribute to headline CPI dynamics?
  • What is the pass-through from exchange-rate and import-price shocks to domestic prices, and how does it vary over time?
  • How do policy rates, liquidity and credit conditions relate to inflation dynamics?
  • Do inflation persistence and shock transmission differ across regimes or structural-break periods?
  • How do nominal wages and household purchasing power respond to persistent price changes?

Measurement and data

The primary price measure is the CPI published by Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics (BBS); monetary, exchange-rate and related macroeconomic series are available through Bangladesh Bank, Economic Data and the Bangladesh National Summary Data Page. The IMF's Bangladesh national-accounts metadata illustrates why source definitions, compilation practices and revisions should be examined before analysis [IMF, Bangladesh DQAF; IMF, Bangladesh national-accounts metadata].

Researchers must record CPI base year, basket and weighting changes, geographic coverage, frequency, seasonal adjustment and the treatment of missing observations. Series spanning a rebasing or methodological revision should not be spliced mechanically without documenting the linking procedure and its implications.

Analytical framework

Inflation analysis can be organised around cost-push, demand, exchange-rate and expectations channels. A multivariate framework may include CPI components, exchange rates, policy rates, money or credit, wages and relevant external price measures. Variable selection should follow a stated transmission mechanism and data availability rather than a mechanical search for statistical significance.

Methods and identification

  • VAR and Bayesian VAR: dynamic interactions and conditional impulse responses under stated identification restrictions.
  • Local projections: horizon-specific responses that allow flexible dynamics and selected forms of heterogeneity.
  • ARDL and error-correction models: short-run dynamics and long-run relationships when integration properties and specification support the approach.
  • Markov-switching and threshold models: changes in persistence or transmission across latent or observed regimes.

Impulse responses are conditional on ordering, restrictions and model specification; cointegration does not by itself establish causality. Robustness should examine alternative price measures, lag lengths, sample windows, break treatments and uncertainty intervals. Model implementations can be explored through Open Econometric Systems, with each publication documenting its own specification.

Policy relevance

Evidence can inform the timing and distribution of disinflation, the interaction between monetary policy and supply-side pressures, and the protection of real incomes. Policy conclusions should distinguish temporary relative-price shocks from broad and persistent inflation and should report distributional implications where household-level evidence is available.
