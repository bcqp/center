Research area

Labor and wages

Employment, real wages and the informal economy.

Why labor and wages matter

Employment, earnings and labor-market transitions connect macroeconomic performance to household welfare. Changes in consumer prices affect real wages, while sectoral demand, technology, trade and public policy influence employment opportunities, hours and job quality. Bangladesh's labor market also requires careful treatment of informal work, unpaid family work and differences between survey concepts.

Research questions

  • How do nominal earnings and real wages evolve across sectors and worker groups?
  • How do inflation shocks affect purchasing power across income distributions?
  • What are the patterns of formal, informal, vulnerable and self-employment?
  • How do trade, energy prices and macroeconomic shocks affect employment and labor reallocation?
  • Do employment and wage responses differ by gender, age, education, location or industry?

Data and measurement

Primary labor-market sources include BBS labor-force surveys and wage statistics [Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics (BBS)]. International comparisons may use ILO, ILOSTAT, but definitions, age thresholds, reference periods and labor-force concepts must be aligned before comparison. Household welfare and development indicators can be cross-checked through World Bank, World Development Indicators.

Employment-to-population ratios, unemployment, labor-force participation, hours worked, earnings and informal employment measure different dimensions. Researchers should record survey design, weights, strata, clusters, reference periods, nonresponse and classification changes. Real wages require an explicitly selected price deflator and matching time frequency.

Analytical framework

BIQEP links labor demand, wage setting, worker composition and household purchasing power. Aggregate wage indices may conceal shifts in the composition of employment; microdata can help separate within-group wage changes from changes in the types of workers employed, subject to access and sample design.

Methods and identification

  • Survey-weighted descriptive analysis: labor-market levels and distributional profiles.
  • Quantile regression: heterogeneous conditional associations across the earnings distribution.
  • Panel and repeated-cross-section methods: group and time variation, subject to comparability of survey waves.
  • Local projections or ARDL: aggregate dynamic relationships when frequency and integration properties support them.

Causal claims require a credible source of exogenous variation or a defensible quasi-experimental design. Quantile coefficients are not automatically causal effects. Standard errors should respect survey design or panel dependence, and results should report sensitivity to deflators, sample restrictions and occupational classifications.

Policy relevance

Research can inform minimum-wage discussions, social protection, skills policy, labor-market formalisation and the distributional consequences of macroeconomic adjustment. Conclusions should distinguish wage levels, wage growth, employment quantity and job quality.
