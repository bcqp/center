Research area

Monetary policy

Policy rates, transmission to lending and the exchange rate.

Why monetary policy matters

Monetary policy affects aggregate demand and inflation through a sequence of linked markets: policy instruments influence money-market conditions, bank funding costs, lending and deposit rates, credit supply, asset prices and exchange-rate expectations. The strength and timing of these channels are empirical questions and may vary with banking conditions, market structure and the policy regime.

Research questions

  • How rapidly do policy-rate changes pass through to money-market, deposit and lending rates?
  • How do credit, output and prices respond over different horizons?
  • Does exchange-rate flexibility alter the transmission of domestic and external shocks?
  • Has transmission changed across monetary-policy frameworks or periods of financial stress?
  • Do transmission effects differ across bank types, sectors or borrower groups?

Data and institutional sources

Policy announcements and monetary and financial statistics are documented by Bangladesh Bank, Economic Data. The Bangladesh National Summary Data Page provides standardised access to interest rates, monetary aggregates, CPI, exchange rates and other macroeconomic domains. IMF country metadata provides additional documentation on statistical definitions and dissemination practices [IMF, DSBB; IMF Dissemination Standards Bulletin Board].

Transmission framework

BIQEP examines the interest-rate, bank-lending, credit, exchange-rate and expectations channels. A coherent empirical design should distinguish the policy instrument from intermediate financial conditions and final outcomes. It should also account for the possibility that policy responds endogenously to inflation and output developments.

Methods and identification

  • Structural VARs: dynamic responses under explicit contemporaneous or long-run restrictions.
  • Bayesian VARs: regularised multivariate dynamics where parameter dimensionality is large relative to the sample.
  • Time-varying parameter models: evolving transmission coefficients across changing financial and policy environments.
  • Local projections: flexible horizon-specific responses and subgroup analysis.

Identification requires a defensible strategy for separating policy innovations from systematic policy responses. Recursive ordering, sign restrictions, external instruments or narrative information imply different assumptions and should be justified. Standard errors or posterior intervals, stability diagnostics, alternative identification schemes and sample sensitivity should accompany the results.

Interpretation and policy relevance

Estimated transmission is conditional on the policy regime, market structure and sample period. A rate change need not have uniform effects across borrowers or banks. BIQEP therefore treats aggregate responses and distributional or sectoral heterogeneity as complementary questions rather than interchangeable evidence.
