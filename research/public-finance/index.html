Research area

Public finance

Revenue, spending, deficits and debt sustainability.

Why public finance matters

Revenue mobilisation, expenditure composition, deficits, financing and public debt shape macroeconomic stability and the capacity to provide public services. Fiscal analysis must distinguish budget estimates from realised execution, central from general government coverage, cash from accrual recording and nominal amounts from real or GDP-scaled measures.

Research questions

  • How do tax and non-tax revenues respond to changes in output, imports and prices?
  • How does expenditure composition evolve across recurrent spending, investment and social protection?
  • What are the fiscal and macroeconomic implications of subsidies and administered prices?
  • How sensitive are debt and debt-service indicators to growth, interest rates and exchange-rate assumptions?
  • What are the short-run output and price effects of revenue and spending shocks?

Data and fiscal definitions

Budget documents, fiscal tables and source directories are available through the Finance Division, Ministry of Finance, data-source directory. Revenue information may also be drawn from the National Board of Revenue, while cross-domain macroeconomic series can be checked against the Bangladesh National Summary Data Page. The IMF's government-finance metadata and dissemination resources should be consulted when assessing institutional coverage and classification [IMF, DSBB; IMF Dissemination Standards Bulletin Board].

Every analysis should identify the fiscal year, institutional perimeter, accounting basis, budget stage and classification system. Budget allocation, revised budget, actual expenditure and cash execution are different statistical objects. Debt measures should specify instrument coverage, currency denomination, residency and valuation basis.

Analytical framework

BIQEP considers fiscal cyclicality, revenue elasticity, expenditure multipliers, fiscal-monetary interactions, debt dynamics and distributional incidence. Debt sustainability scenarios should state the assumptions for real growth, effective interest rates, primary balances, exchange rates and contingent liabilities. Scenario projections are conditional exercises, not unconditional forecasts.

Methods and identification

  • Local projections and structural VARs: dynamic responses to identified fiscal innovations.
  • Bayesian VARs: multivariate fiscal-macro dynamics with regularisation.
  • Threshold and regime models: nonlinear responses across debt, output-gap or financing conditions.
  • Debt-dynamics accounting: transparent decomposition of interest-growth differentials, primary balances and stock-flow adjustments.

Fiscal shocks are difficult to identify because taxes and spending respond to the cycle. Research should distinguish discretionary policy changes from automatic stabilisers and anticipated measures. Alternative shock definitions, institutional coverage, fiscal-year alignment and uncertainty analysis are central robustness checks.

Policy relevance

Findings can inform revenue reform, spending efficiency, social protection, subsidy design and debt management. Recommendations should make explicit the distributional consequences, implementation constraints and macroeconomic assumptions underlying the analysis.
